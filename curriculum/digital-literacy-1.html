Being Kind Online

Intro to Digital Literacy · Module 1 of 4

Goal: recognize how small choices online affect real people

What is digital citizenship?

Being a good digital citizen means treating people online the same way you'd treat them in person — even though you usually can't see their face or hear their voice. It's easy to forget there's a real person behind every screen name, which is exactly why online spaces need more intentional kindness, not less.

What kindness looks like online

Instead of…Try…
Piling on in the comments of a group chat argumentSending a private message to check in, or simply not engaging
Screenshotting a private conversation to share elsewhereAsking permission first, or just not sharing it
Staying silent while someone is being bullied onlineReporting it, or reaching out to the person being targeted
Assuming the worst about a short or blunt textAsking what someone meant before reacting

Why it matters

Because online interactions can happen fast and reach a lot of people, unkind moments online can spread further and last longer than an in-person conflict — but the same is true for kindness. A supportive comment, a shared resource, or simply not piling on can matter just as much as anything you'd do face-to-face.

Play & Learn: Kind Kingdom

Google's free browser game Interland has a level built around exactly this. No download or account needed.

  1. Open Interland and click Play.
  2. From the map, choose the golden castle: Kind Kingdom.
  3. Play through the level (about 5–10 minutes) and see if you can earn the "You're Internet Kind" badge.

Reflect & Submit

After you finish Kind Kingdom, jot down short answers to these and submit them below:

  • What's one choice in the game that surprised you?
  • Describe one situation from your own life (school, group chat, gaming) where this lesson could apply.
