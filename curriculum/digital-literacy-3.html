Secure Your Secrets

Intro to Digital Literacy · Module 3 of 4

Goal: build strong passwords and understand basic account security

Why passwords matter

A weak or reused password is one of the easiest ways for someone to get into your accounts — email, social media, school portals. Once they're in one account, attackers often try the same password everywhere else, which is why reusing passwords is riskier than it seems.

What makes a password strong

WeakWhy it's weak
password123Common word + predictable numbers
yourname2010Based on public info (name, birth year)
123456One of the most guessed passwords in the world

Stronger passwords are long, unpredictable, and unique per account. A random string of unrelated words, like correct-horse-lantern-42, is both easier to remember and harder to guess than a short "clever" one. A password manager (often built into your phone or browser) can also generate and remember strong passwords for you.

Two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA adds a second step to logging in — usually a code sent to your phone or an app. Even if someone steals your password, they can't get in without that second code. Turning on 2FA for your email and social accounts is one of the single most effective things you can do to stay secure.
