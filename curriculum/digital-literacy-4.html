Don't Fall for Fake

Intro to Digital Literacy · Module 4 of 4 (Capstone)

Goal: spot misinformation, scams, and phishing before they spot you

Not everything online is true

Anyone can publish anything online, which means false information can look just as polished as real information — sometimes more so. This module ties together everything from the last three: being thoughtful about what you share (Module 2) and keeping your accounts secure (Module 3) both depend on being able to tell real from fake in the first place.

Quick checks before you trust or share something

Red flagWhat to do instead
A headline designed to make you angry or scaredSearch the claim on a second, trusted source before reacting
An urgent message asking you to click a link or "verify your account"Go to the site directly by typing the address yourself, not the link
An account with no history, no profile photo, pushing one messageCheck who's behind it before trusting what it says
"Too good to be true" prizes, giveaways, or dealsAssume it's a scam until proven otherwise

Play & Learn: Reality River

Last stop on Interland.

  1. From the map, choose Reality River.
  2. Play through the level (about 5–10 minutes), deciding what's real and what's fake as you cross.
  3. See if you can earn the "You're Internet Smart" badge — and claim your Certificate of Awesomeness if you've completed all four kingdoms!

Want more practice?

The FBI's Safe Online Surfing program and Common Sense Media's internet safety resources (also cited in our safety blogs) are great follow-ups if you want to go deeper.
